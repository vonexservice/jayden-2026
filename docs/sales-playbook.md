# Local Sales and Rental Inquiry Playbook

## Target customer segments

Prioritize businesses whose work plausibly requires short-term equipment or trailer rentals:
- Landscaping and hardscaping contractors
- Excavation, grading, trenching, and site-preparation crews
- Fence, deck, patio, and paving contractors
- Home builders and renovation companies
- Property maintenance and acreage service providers
- Small construction companies and independent trades
- Local organizations with occasional hauling or site-work needs

## Qualification

Before outreach or quoting, determine:
- Type of job and equipment needed
- Desired rental dates and duration
- Delivery or pickup preference and destination
- Operator experience and any relevant operating requirements
- Trailer towing vehicle and hitch requirements where relevant
- Availability, current rate, deposits, damage waiver, fuel/cleaning terms, and applicable taxes
- Whether a repeat rental or account relationship is likely

Never promise availability, a rate, delivery coverage, or technical capacity until verified with the business.

## Prospecting workflow

1. Build a small, relevant prospect list from legitimate public business sources.
2. Record company name, public business contact channel, segment, location, fit reason, source URL, date checked, and status.
3. Personalize outreach around the prospect's actual work, not generic bulk messaging.
4. Offer a useful next step: check availability, discuss the right equipment, or prepare a quote.
5. Log responses, opt-outs, follow-up date, quote status, and outcome.
6. Stop contacting anyone who opts out; comply with applicable Canadian anti-spam and privacy rules.
7. Review which segments produce qualified inquiries and repeat bookings.

## Simple pipeline stages

- Identified
- Fit checked
- Contacted
- Replied
- Requirement qualified
- Quote requested
- Quote sent
- Booking confirmed
- Lost / not a fit
- Follow-up later

## Outreach principles

- Be concise, relevant, and human.
- Do not imply an existing relationship where none exists.
- Avoid mass scraping and indiscriminate email blasts.
- Use a clear identity and contact details, and provide a compliant opt-out where required.
- Prefer permission-based follow-up and useful service information over pressure tactics.

## Weekly sales review

- New qualified prospects
- Replies and positive responses
- Rental requirements identified
- Quotes requested/sent
- Confirmed bookings and estimated revenue
- Repeat-rental opportunities
- Reasons for lost opportunities
